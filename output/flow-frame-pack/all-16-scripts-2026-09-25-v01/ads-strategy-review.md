## Problem → scent answer: applied script revision

The owner requested a question that makes the buying decision recognizable, followed by a relevant fragrance answer. The revised 16 scripts use **buyer question → named scent or product answer → specific scent/personality → physical details → offer → CTA**. The separate 44-second brand introduction preserves “Hi, kami ang Dear Body,” then immediately asks “Hirap pumili ng scent?”

For Dear Body, the problem is choosing a scent or finding a preferred scent profile. The answer is a clearly described option—not a promise to change someone’s personality. Example: “Rose ang gusto mo, pero may smoky depth sana?” → “Try ang Oud Mirage ng Dear Body.” → the approved dark-rose, smoky-woody description and independent/reflective/self-assured fit. The magnetic cap and collectible/displayable packaging then add concrete reasons to consider the product.

This adapts the relatable problem/product-answer structure described in [TikTok’s official creative guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/guides/what-is-ad-creative-guide), and varies customer motivations in the spirit of [Meta Blueprint’s creative-diversification guidance](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/activity/707402). It is a creative hypothesis for Facebook testing, not a measured Dear Body result or evidence that a TikTok tactic will necessarily perform the same on Meta.

**One-variable hook test:** for one scent, compare the supplied question with a direct introduction such as “Kilalanin ang Oud Mirage ng Dear Body.” Keep the subsequent script, voice, duration, product, offer, destination and visual treatment the same. Judge purchase outcomes and delivered COD economics; use watch time and clicks to diagnose the opening. A different hook for a different scent is not a controlled hook test.

## What the deeper research changes

| Earlier approach | Version 2 recommendation | Why this is a better question to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead discovery with broad personality adjectives | Connect personality to an understandable scent preference | A viewer can decide whether creamy-fruity or smoky-spiced sounds appealing, even without smelling it |
| Use the two-item pair as a main cold-audience concept | Make one scent easy to choose; present the pair as an optional order-value incentive | A first-time buyer may hesitate to commit to two unfamiliar bottles. This is a risk hypothesis, not measured Dear Body behavior |
| Mostly polished lifestyle video | Mix a scent-selection edit, a clear product-description still/carousel, and a real hands-only demonstration | These offer different information and viewing experiences; changing only the bottle and background is a narrower test |
| Magnetic cap as a standalone selling idea | Use cap/packaging detail to support confidence in the product | A cap can demonstrate a physical detail, but it cannot tell someone whether they will enjoy the scent |
| Explain every shopping click in each ad | Give one ad CTA; put detailed ordering steps in the site and a separate how-to creative | The scent message gets more attention; interested shoppers still receive exact ordering instructions |
| Judge early winners by the overall ad set | Separate exploratory delivery from a controlled comparison | Low delivery is not proof an ad is bad; changing several variables prevents a clean explanation of why it worked |

**The latest video-script direction leads with the buyer question.** The offer now supports the product answer and closes the main scripts: free shipping on 2+ items, then COD and the appropriate CTA. The standalone brand introduction keeps a brief brand greeting before its question. Existing offer-led static ads remain separate creatives.

## Make the landing page answer the same question

1. **Selection ad:** same scent choices and approved descriptions at the destination. Measure whether the finder helps people reach a relevant product and order.
2. **Single-scent ad:** land on that exact product, showing bottle size, current price/stock, scent description, photos and ordering action.
3. **Product demonstration:** show the same actual packaging and an accurate feature description. Add genuine reviews later when available and permissioned.
4. **Pair offer:** make quantity and the two-item shipping threshold clear, while retaining the option to order one bottle.

Show shipping terms near selection/ordering as well as in the banner. Baymard's research supports making costs visible early; it does not tell us what Dear Body's conversion rate will be. [Baymard product-page shipping research](https://baymard.com/research-articles/avoid-banners-only-free-shipping).

The owner confirms that the website button says **Order Now**. Use that wording in purchase scripts and creative; it supersedes the labels in the older local-theme snapshot. Confirm the live one-item charge, free-shipping eligibility, stock and COD total before paid launch. This research revision does not change the website or checkout.

## The next experiment depends on what actually fails

| Observation | Next useful change | Avoid concluding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Very little ad delivery | Check eligibility, budget and allocation; reserve a controlled test if the idea deserves one | “This hook does not work” |
| People watch but few reach the site | Test a clearer scent choice and CTA with the same product/format | “We need more beautiful footage” |
| Clicks but few loaded landing visits | Check mobile loading, ad destination and redirects | “The scent is unpopular” |
| Finder use but few product visits/orders | Examine result usefulness and test a more direct product/collection route | “Quiz completions prove purchase intent” |
| Product interest but little ordering | Check price, scent clarity, real product evidence and transparent shipping | “We must force a two-bottle bundle” |
| Form openings but few submitted orders | Check COD form behavior, unexpected totals and fields | “The creative is the only problem” |
| Submitted orders but many failures | Improve order confirmation and fulfillment, then recalculate allowable acquisition cost | “Reported purchase ROAS equals collected profit” |

The first campaign is an exploratory sales test. For a controlled creative comparison, use an available A/B testing workflow with one changed variable, a pre-agreed test allowance, a consistent attribution window and time for COD outcomes to mature. A two-ad test receiving unequal algorithmic spend is not automatically a fair experiment. Do not declare a winner from a single order or scale from views alone.

## Tracking: check the COD path before spending

The theme explicitly hands orders to EasySell, and its app embed is enabled in the local configuration. This does not tell us which Meta dataset is connected or whether the current live events work.

Shopify supports Pixel and Conversions API data sharing and describes standard shopping events. A custom COD flow needs its own validation rather than assuming the normal checkout path covers it. Review the chosen sharing level and the store's customer disclosure alongside setup. [Shopify data sharing documentation](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/promoting-marketing/analyze-marketing/meta-data-sharing).

EasySell documents a configurable Purchase/Lead conversion event, matching browser/server event IDs, and optional browser funnel events. Its CAPI does not send every upper-funnel event. It also warns about duplicate purchases when another app separately tracks the same EasySell order. Verify one coordinated tracking setup. [EasySell tracking FAQ](https://help.tyslo.com/en/article/meta-pixel-conversions-api-capi-faq-1sfm23z/), [EasySell duplicate-event guidance](https://help.tyslo.com/en/article/double-meta-purchase-events-potential-causes-1p7sjyf/).

Before launch, check one controlled test order through Events Manager and the store's order records:

1. A product visit is distinguishable from a selection, checkout/form opening and completed order submission.
2. Opening the COD form or adding a second item never counts as a completed Purchase.
3. One submitted order resolves to one deduplicated Purchase with the expected order value and PHP currency. Closing/reopening or refreshing confirmation must not create a new purchase.
4. The ad campaign, creative and landing page can be identified through consistent UTM parameters and the store's available attribution data. Keep personal information out of URLs.
5. The business records submitted, confirmed, cancelled, delivered and returned COD orders separately.

These are recommended checks, not tests completed in this review. No test order was submitted.

## Budget and what counts as success

Your daily budget, product costs, fulfillment costs and delivery success rate are still unknown. Choose a **total test amount you can afford to lose**, then divide it by the planned number of days. For illustration only, a ₱3,500 seven-day allowance averages ₱500 per day; this is not a required minimum, a sales forecast or an approved spend.

Set an allowable acquisition cost from actual unit economics:

**Allowable ad cost per submitted COD order = (delivery rate × contribution per delivered order) − ((1 − delivery rate) × average failed-order cost) − desired profit reserve per submitted order.**

Contribution means money collected minus product cost, packaging, shipping subsidy, COD/payment fees and other variable fulfillment costs, before advertising. Failed-order cost includes the actual expected handling and return costs; avoid counting costs twice. Compute single-item and 2+ item orders separately because their shipping economics differ. If the result is zero or negative, improve the offer economics before buying traffic.

Use these business measures to judge the launch:

| Measure | How to read it |
| --- | --- |
| Ad spend ÷ attributed submitted orders | Early acquisition cost; still includes COD failures |
| Ad spend ÷ attributed delivered, paid orders | More useful final acquisition cost; report only after delivery outcomes mature |
| Collected revenue, variable costs and ad spend | Determines contribution after advertising |
| Two-or-more-item share and average collected order value | Shows whether the shipping offer grows order value profitably |
| Clicks → landing visits → product/finder actions → submitted orders | Locates friction; these are diagnostic signals rather than the final success criterion |

Use the same order cohort and attribution convention when comparing spend and delivered results. Meta-reported purchases and ROAS can reflect submitted COD orders before payment is collected. Reconcile them with business outcomes rather than assuming they equal cash revenue. This is an accounting implication of COD, not a finding that your account currently misreports.

## First two weeks

| Period | Work and decision |
| --- | --- |
| Before launch | Verify mobile ordering, pricing/stock, offer conditions, placement previews and deduplicated order events. Set the total spend cap and allowable cost per order |
| Days 1–3 | Start the small creative set. Correct broken links, tracking or delivery immediately. Observe where visitors drop out; avoid frequent cosmetic changes |
| Days 4–7 | Compare spend, orders and order quality by concept. Give under-delivered ads no winner/loser label. Investigate ads consuming the pre-agreed test allowance without purchases before adding spend |
| Days 8–14 | Keep promising concepts, introduce one revised hook or one next concept, and reconcile mature delivery outcomes. Extend observation if COD delivery results are still pending |
| After the first reliable results | Increase budget gradually only when tracking, stock, fulfillment and contribution support it. Keep testing distinct messages; add remarketing only when the audience supports it |

An ad set distributes spend unevenly, so this first round is exploratory. Use a controlled A/B test when you need a stronger causal comparison, and avoid changing audience, scent, hook and landing page together in that test. There is no universal CTR or two-day rule that proves a fragrance ad is profitable.
